\begin{abstract}
Positional encoding controls how transformers generalize past their training length. We train nine comparable architectures ($\sim$17.6M parameters) from scratch on WikiText-103 at a fixed context length of 512 tokens, varying only the positional encoding: Learned Absolute, Sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi, KERPLE, CABLE, NoPE, and two inference-time RoPE extensions (Position Interpolation, YaRN). We measure perplexity, needle retrieval accuracy, and inference latency from 512 to 8,192 tokens ($16\times$ the training length). ALiBi and CABLE remain near-perfectly stable through extrapolation (ratio $\approx$1.0), while NoPE (1.27$\times$), KERPLE (1.30$\times$), and YaRN (1.59$\times$) degrade moderately, Learned (1.76$\times$) and RoPE (2.11$\times$) degrade sharply, and Sinusoidal fails catastrophically ($>$800$\times$). Needle retrieval scores 0\% across all models, indicating a capacity-bound failure independent of positional encoding. ALiBi's stability comes at a cost: its precomputed attention bias buffer consumes 1.26\,GB of VRAM, $6.5\times$ the memory footprint of the other methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Transformer architectures are the standard foundation for natural language processing \cite{vaswani2017,touvron2023}. Self-attention models dependencies across sequence tokens, but it treats inputs as unordered sets without positional information \cite{vaswani2017}.

Positional encodings assign position representations to tokens. Early models used absolute encodings: fixed sinusoidal functions or learned position embeddings \cite{vaswani2017,devlin2019,radford2019}. These work within the training window but degrade rapidly on longer sequences \cite{press2022}.

Recent architectures use relative rotations or attention score biases to address this. Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) rotates query and key vectors in complex space \cite{su2024roformer}. Attention with Linear Biases (ALiBi) adds distance-based penalties directly to attention logits \cite{press2022}. Several RoPE extensions have been proposed for long-context deployment: Position Interpolation (PI) compresses the position scale to fit longer sequences \cite{chen2023}, and YaRN applies NTK-aware frequency scaling \cite{peng2023yarn}. KERPLE and CABLE apply learned bias functions over pairwise distances, while NoPE ablates positional encoding entirely \cite{kazemnejad2023,cable2023}.

This paper compares nine positional encoding methods under controlled conditions. We keep model size, training data, and architecture fixed, training comparable transformers ($\sim$17.6M parameters) from scratch on WikiText-103 at a fixed context length of 512 tokens, differing only in their positional encoding. PI and YaRN are evaluated as inference-time extensions of the trained RoPE checkpoint with no additional training.

The contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A controlled experiment isolating the positional encoding as the sole variable across nine methods, evaluated at context lengths from 512 to 8,192 tokens ($16\times$ the training length).
\item Evidence that extrapolation stability spans a continuum: from near-perfect stability (ALiBi, CABLE) through moderate degradation (NoPE, KERPLE, YaRN) to catastrophic failure (Sinusoidal), with Learned and RoPE in between.
\item Quantification of ALiBi's VRAM overhead ($1.26$\,GB vs.\ $\sim$195\,MB for all others) due to its precomputed bias buffer.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Extrapolation and Design Trade-offs}

Absolute encodings extrapolate worst. Learned embeddings have no representation past the training length \cite{devlin2019,radford2019,press2022}. Sinusoidal encoding is defined at any position but rarely generalizes, because models do not learn its underlying structure \cite{vaswani2017,press2022}. RoPE extrapolates further by encoding distance as continuous rotation, but its high-frequency dimensions oscillate beyond the training range \cite{su2024roformer,chen2023}. ALiBi extrapolates best among standard methods because its linear penalty is defined by construction at any pairwise distance \cite{press2022}. KERPLE generalizes this to learned kernel biases \cite{kerple2022}. CABLE learns a linear attention bias through a small shared network \cite{cable2023}. NoPE omits positional encoding entirely, relying on the causal mask's implicit ordering \cite{kazemnejad2023}.

PI and YaRN do not train new models. They rescale the RoPE frequencies so that the trained window maps onto a longer target length at inference time. PI divides position indices by a scale factor \cite{chen2023}; YaRN blends NTK-aware frequency scaling with attention scaling \cite{peng2023yarn}.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Theoretical Framework and Justification}

Self-attention compares query and key vectors using similarity scores. Without positional encoding, self-attention treats inputs as unordered sets \cite{vaswani2017}. Positional encodings break this symmetry by injecting position information into attention.

The nine methods we evaluate group into paradigm families:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Absolute}: Learned embedding vectors added to input tokens; fixed Sinusoidal functions.
\item \textbf{Rotary (RoPE)}: Complex rotations applied to query and key vectors \cite{su2024roformer}. PI and YaRN extend RoPE for long-context inference.
\item \textbf{Bias-based (ALiBi family)}: Distance penalties added to attention logits \cite{press2022}. KERPLE and CABLE are learned variants.
\item \textbf{Implicit}: NoPE trains without any positional mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research Design}
We use a two-part empirical design: a \emph{controlled experiment} isolating the positional encoding as the sole variable, and a supplementary zero-shot evaluation of pre-trained models.

For the controlled experiment, we evaluate at context lengths $L \in \{512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Perplexity Extrapolation}: Sliding-window perplexity on held-out text (stride $=$ context length).
\item \textbf{Needle-in-a-Haystack Retrieval}: Target placed at depths 0\%, 25\%, 50\%, 75\%, 100\%. A unique five-digit code is inserted into a haystack of filler text; the model must reproduce it exactly after the prompt ``What is the secret code? Answer:''. Three trials per depth; accuracy is the fraction of exact matches.
\item \textbf{Inference Overhead}: Per-token latency (ms/token) and VRAM at the training context length.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Data Collection Procedure}
The controlled experiment trains on WikiText-103 (GPT-2 tokenizer, vocab 50,257) at a fixed context length of 512 tokens for 10,000 steps (AdamW, lr $3{\times}10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.1, warmup 1,000, cosine decay, fp16, seed 42). Batch size is 16 (micro-batch 8, gradient accumulation 2). We evaluate validation perplexity using sliding windows at each length.

\subsection{Data Processing and Analysis Tools}
The controlled experiment runs in PyTorch with fp16 mixed precision on a single NVIDIA T4 GPU (16\,GB). The zero-shot benchmark runs using PyTorch and Hugging Face \texttt{transformers}.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Controlled Experiment}
\label{sec:controlled}

\paragraph{Setup.} Seven transformer architectures ($\sim$17.6M parameters: $d{=}256$, 6 layers, 4 heads, $d_{\text{head}}{=}64$, $d_{\text{ff}}{=}1024$, vocab 50,257) train from scratch on WikiText-103 at a fixed context length of 512 tokens. The only difference between models is the positional encoding: Learned Absolute, Sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi, KERPLE, CABLE, or NoPE. Position Interpolation and YaRN are evaluated as inference-time extensions of the trained RoPE checkpoint with no additional training. KERPLE adds only $+4$ parameters; CABLE adds $+2{,}081$ ($\ll$0.1\% of the model). All reported values come from the best validation perplexity checkpoint saved during training.

At $L{=}512$ (within the training window), KERPLE achieves the lowest perplexity (329.0), followed by RoPE (336.7) and ALiBi (358.9). Sinusoidal (356.0) and Learned (360.2) are comparable; NoPE (367.0), CABLE (377.7), and YaRN (401.0) are somewhat higher. PI (632.0) reflects position compression: PI maps the 8,192-token eval range onto the 512-token training range by dividing position indices by 16, so even at $L{=}512$ the model sees compressed positions $[0, 1/16, \ldots, 511/16]$ rather than $[0, 1, \ldots, 511]$, degrading in-window perplexity in exchange for extrapolation headroom.

Beyond the training window, the methods diverge. Table~\ref{tab:cost} shows the extrapolation ratio (PPL at $L{=}8192$ divided by PPL at $L{=}512$). ALiBi and CABLE are essentially flat (ratio $\approx$1.0): ALiBi's static distance penalty $m_h \cdot |i{-}j|$ is defined at any pairwise distance by construction, so longer contexts leave the attention profile unchanged. CABLE's learned linear bias achieves the same effect through a small shared network. NoPE (1.27$\times$) and KERPLE (1.30$\times$) degrade mildly. YaRN (1.59$\times$) degrades moderately---its NTK-aware frequency blending partially preserves high-frequency information but does not fully resolve extrapolation. Learned (1.76$\times$) and RoPE (2.11$\times$) degrade sharply: Learned has no representation beyond position 511, and RoPE's high-frequency dimensions oscillate beyond the training range. Sinusoidal fails catastrophically (822$\times$): while sinusoidal functions are defined at any position, the model never learns to use this structure, and attention scores collapse at unseen positions.

\paragraph{Needle retrieval.} All nine methods score 0\% retrieval accuracy at every context depth and every evaluated length (Figure~\ref{fig:needle}). This uniform failure is capacity-bound: at 17.6M parameters, the model lacks the capacity to reliably retrieve specific information from long contexts regardless of positional encoding. The same task succeeds on larger pre-trained models (124M--559M parameters) within their training windows \cite{press2022}. We caution against interpreting needle results at this scale as evidence about positional encoding extrapolation.

\paragraph{Inference cost.} Table~\ref{tab:cost} reports latency and VRAM. All methods except ALiBi have comparable VRAM ($\sim$195\,MB) and latency ($\sim$0.015\,ms/token). ALiBi's precomputed bias tensor ($4 \times 8192 \times 8192$ float32 for 4 heads) consumes 1.26\,GB, a $6.5\times$ overhead. This is a fixed architectural cost that scales with the maximum evaluated sequence length, not a training cost.

\subsection{Limitations}

Several limitations constrain interpretation. First, all controlled models are small ($\sim$17.6M parameters); extrapolation behavior may differ at larger scales where models have sufficient capacity to learn positional structure. Second, we evaluate only on WikiText-103; results may differ on other domains. Third, PI and YaRN inherit the RoPE checkpoint's training trajectory, so their evaluation is confounded by the base model's quality. Fourth, Sinusoidal's catastrophic failure at unseen positions may reflect insufficient training rather than an inherent limitation of sinusoidal functions; a longer training schedule could potentially change this result.

\section{Discussion}

The nine-method comparison reveals a clear hierarchy of extrapolation stability:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Near-perfect stability} (ratio $\approx$1.0): ALiBi and CABLE. Both use distance-based biases defined at any pairwise position by construction. ALiBi's bias is fixed (no learned parameters); CABLE's is learned through a small network.
\item \textbf{Mild degradation} (1.1--1.3$\times$): PI, NoPE, and KERPLE. PI trades in-window quality for extrapolation headroom via position compression. NoPE's surprisingly good extrapolation (1.27$\times$) suggests the causal mask provides implicit positional information sufficient for moderate extrapolation.
\item \textbf{Moderate degradation} (1.3--2.1$\times$): YaRN, Learned, and RoPE. YaRN's NTK-aware blending helps but does not fully resolve extrapolation. Learned embeddings have no representation past position 511. RoPE's high-frequency dimensions oscillate beyond the training range.
\item \textbf{Catastrophic failure} ($>$800$\times$): Sinusoidal. Despite being mathematically defined at any position, the model fails to learn the sinusoidal structure at this scale.
\end{enumerate}

The cost--stability trade-off is most pronounced for ALiBi: it achieves the best extrapolation stability but requires $6.5\times$ more VRAM than all other methods due to its precomputed bias buffer. For deployment scenarios with tight memory budgets, CABLE offers comparable stability at standard memory cost, while NoPE eliminates positional encoding overhead entirely at the cost of modest degradation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We compared nine positional encoding methods trained from scratch on WikiText-103: Learned Absolute, Sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi, KERPLE, CABLE, NoPE, Position Interpolation, and YaRN. Our controlled experiment isolates the positional encoding as the sole variable at 17.6M parameters, evaluated up to $16\times$ the training context length.

Three findings emerge. First, extrapolation stability spans a wide continuum: ALiBi and CABLE remain essentially flat (ratio $\approx$1.0), while Sinusoidal fails catastrophically ($>$800$\times$), with all other methods falling between these extremes. Second, needle retrieval fails uniformly (0\%) at this model scale, indicating a capacity-bound failure independent of positional encoding. Third, ALiBi's extrapolation stability comes at a VRAM cost ($1.26$\,GB) that is $6.5\times$ the footprint of all other methods.

These findings confirm that ALiBi provides length extrapolation without fine-tuning in this setup, but also reveal that the extrapolation landscape is richer than a simple three-way comparison suggests: NoPE, KERPLE, and CABLE offer alternative trade-off points between stability, in-window performance, and memory cost.
